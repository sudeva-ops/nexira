## 2. Gaps and conflicts, with proposed answers

| # | Gap | Proposed answer |
|---|---|---|
| G-1 | **Sources.** Visible Earth now redirects to `science.nasa.gov`. | Checked on 2026-11-12 under `assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/esd/eo/images/`:<br>• Blue Marble Next Generation with topography and bathymetry, December 2004, whole world: `bmng/bmng-topography-bathymetry/december/world.topo.bathy.200412.3x21600x10800.jpg`, 21600 × 10800, about 2 km a pixel, 29.9 MB.<br>• Black Marble 2016 colour: `imagerecords/144000/144898/BlackMarble_2016_3km.jpg`, 13500 × 6750, 8.1 MB.<br>Both are pinned by SHA-256 in the script. NASA imagery is generally not under copyright, and the credit line will be "Imagery: NASA Earth Observatory". The PR records the NASA media-use guidelines it relied on. |
| G-2 | **The raster tooling.** There is no GDAL on the build machine. | The script runs GDAL from its official container image (`ghcr.io/osgeo/gdal`), pinned by digest and run only at build time. The steps are:<br>1. Georeference with `gdal_translate -a_srs EPSG:4326 -a_ullr -180 90 180 -90`.<br>2. Reproject with `gdalwarp` to EPSG:3857, clipped at ±85.05°.<br>3. Make MBTiles with WebP tiles and overviews.<br>4. Convert with `pmtiles convert` (the binary the script already pins).<br>The estimate is about 1,365 tiles per layer and 20–60 MB in all. **As built in Prompt 143** (each a deviation for approval in its PR):
- **G-4, no city lights.** MapLibre's raster paint has no mask, and a fill pattern is screen-aligned rather than placed on the map, so plain style layers cannot limit Black Marble to the night side. The night side keeps today's shade over Blue Marble (D-55). `make tiles` builds only `earth-day.pmtiles` (5.7 MB, zoom 0–5, 1,365 WebP tiles), not an unused night file.
- **G-5, the band outside the rim was not a map layer.** It was the radar sweep (`.nx-mapwrap::after`, from UI 3): a rotating gradient over the whole map box. With every MapLibre layer hidden, it was still there. Now the gradient's angle turns instead of the element (`@property --nx-sweep-angle`), and the element is clipped to the globe's rim (`map/three.ts` `clipSweep`). The rim is measured, not estimated: the camera is a perspective one, so the 60° estimate the sign-in globe uses came out about 28 px too large. In the flat view, and closer in, the sweep is not clipped. Replay is flat only and is unchanged.
- **G-1, the NASA terms.** NASA's guidelines, read on 2026-11-12 at `nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/`, say NASA content is "generally not subject to copyright in the United States". NASA should be acknowledged as the source, and commercial use must not imply NASA's endorsement. The map credits "Imagery: NASA Earth Observatory" and uses no NASA insignia. For a commercial release, the guidelines name NASA's brand team as the contact for confirmation.
- **Inland water.** Blue Marble's lakes (Victoria, Tanganyika, Malawi) are very dark once toned down. They are left as the imagery shows them.
